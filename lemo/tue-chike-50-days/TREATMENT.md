# Tuesday: "Chike and the 50 days" (Sketchnote, Manetrust purple)

Format: 1080×1920, 24 fps, 56.6 s, the user's recorded British voiceover, the user's BGM kept low. The screen holds one dot-grid page that is drawn stroke by stroke. The camera rides the pen and ends on a pull-back to the whole page. A small "fictional characters" footer sits on screen for the whole film.

## 1. Three candidate structures
1. **A journey along one line.** One long horizontal timeline, drawn left to right, with Chike's cash coin riding it. The camera follows the pen. Clear, but the "after" has no place to stand next to the "before".
2. **Before and after split down the page.** The left half draws the 50-day cycle, the right half draws the 25-day cycle, the lender sits at the bottom. The comparison is visible, but the two halves have to be small.
3. **A page that reads top to bottom as a poster, with one purple thread.** Block 1 is Chike and his late supplier, block 2 is the question, blocks 3 and 4 are the two cycles stacked so the second sits under the first (same timeline, half the length), block 5 is the cash returning to buy a second depot, block 6 is the lender, block 7 is the call to action. One purple coin follows Chike's cash through every block.

**Chosen: 3.** It matches the script order exactly, it puts both cycles in the same place on the page so the eye does the subtraction, and the purple coin is the single thread. The opening is the number 400 written large on an empty page, not a character shot. The ending is the pull-back poster, with the pen circling "25 days".

## 2. Logline and arc
A busy distributor is always short of cash until he finds the one number that holds it.
Setup: 400 cartons a month, supplier paid late. Turn: the cash conversion cycle is 50 days, and he cuts it to 25. Ending: the cash buys depot two, and a lender can read the numbers.

## 3. Benchmark
- Learn: a live graphic-recording page where each block is planned for the final poster, and numbers get one loop or swash.
- Do not take: any named channel's icons, lettering or layouts.

## 4. Characters
Chike is a fineliner sketch figure (round head, short hair, apron), the only drawn person. The supplier, shop owners and lender appear as icons, speech slips and numbers, never as drawn figures. Poses: standing with a carton, shrugging, pointing, thumbs-up (4 key poses, drawn by code and blended in time).
